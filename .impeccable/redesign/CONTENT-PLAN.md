Source of truth: the new transformation brief in `Pasted text.txt`, attachment `e383bb70-2f85-4baf-98e4-107a95786ac6`. This plan uses that brief alone. It does not import previous portfolio records, contact details, team information, results or brand assets.

## Positioning and content rules

- Company: **Katha Kantam Media**.
- Positioning: **360° Global Creative, Digital Growth & Business Technology Agency**.
- Tagline: **CREATE • MARKET • AUTOMATE • GROW**.
- Primary visitor actions: request a proposal, explore a relevant service, discuss a discovery call or begin a white-label partnership enquiry.
- Core creative, branding, marketing, websites and global production services may be presented as offered services. The proposal still confirms the actual deliverables, cost and schedule.
- AI, CRM, analytics, custom portals and enterprise-style software require consultation before confirming technical feasibility, specialist support or delivery. Do not imply an enterprise implementation team or a completed deployment without evidence.
- Geographic names describe **Markets We Serve**. Never call them offices, branches, established client locations or local teams.
- Do not add awards, client logos, testimonials, client counts, years of experience, performance totals, response-time promises or outcome guarantees without approved evidence.

### 6. Featured case studies / honest interim treatment

No fresh portfolio evidence or approved client records are included in the new brief. Therefore, there is no basis for publishing a factual “Featured Case Studies” section yet.

The Phase 1 review can use the section heading **What a connected brief can look like.** Each item must show a visible **Illustrative approach · not a client project** label. Do not call these client case studies, published work, delivered campaigns or verified outcomes.

Suggested illustrative cards:

1. **A clearer school admissions journey** — an invented scenario about fragmented school information, with a proposed combination of story-led content, an admissions landing page and enquiry routing.
2. **A property launch with a consistent message** — an invented scenario combining campaign creative, a project landing page and a defined lead-capture process.
3. **A repeatable short-form production workflow** — an invented agency-partner scenario covering source files, editing, versions and consolidated review.

For an illustrative item, use these labels:

- **Example challenge:** the hypothetical business problem.
- **Proposed approach:** the recommended combination of capabilities.
- **Example deliverables:** the work that might be included in a scoped engagement.
- **What we would measure:** useful signals to assess after launch, with no claimed result.

Suitable example copy for the school scenario:

> **Example challenge:** Families find isolated posts but no clear explanation of the school or admissions process.
>
> **Proposed approach:** Connect the school story, parent questions and admissions information in one journey.
>
> **Example deliverables:** A messaging direction, short-form content set, admissions landing page and enquiry form.
>
> **What we would measure:** Enquiry sources, completed forms and the admissions team’s assessment of enquiry relevance.

“Outcome” must not mean “expected uplift” or a made-up qualitative success. The review may explicitly state **No project outcome is claimed** when showing an illustrative card.

Once approved evidence is supplied, replace the illustrative section with genuine **Featured Case Studies**, using the required framework: **Client Challenge → Our Work → Delivered Solution → Verified Outcome**. Qualitative outcomes must also be evidenced. If only the delivery scope is verified, say what was delivered and mark business results as **Not provided**. Do not turn the delivery of a website into a claim of increased enquiries.

The `/work/` route follows the same rule. Its division and industry filters may operate on clearly labelled illustrative entries during review, but the page title and introduction must disclose that status. A factual showcase requires fresh approved project assets and evidence; previous project records are excluded.

## Dedicated-page structure

### Service pages

Each of the eight service routes contains:

1. **Business Problem** — a specific need the visitor recognizes.
2. **Our Proposed Solution** — the approach and its scope.
3. **Key Deliverables** — outputs that can be agreed in a proposal.
4. **Four-Step Execution Process** — discover → define → produce/build → review and hand over. Advanced services use discover → assess feasibility → scope a pilot → validate and hand over.
5. **Service-Specific Enquiry CTA** — preselect the capability in the proposal engine.

Technology capabilities disclose consultation status beside the heading and CTA. Media spend, subscriptions, integration charges, maintenance and other ongoing costs are scoped separately; no price or inclusion is assumed from the brief.

### About

Use the supplied positioning, mission direction, four pillars and India-rooted collaboration model. Do not invent a founding year, headcount, leadership biography, credentials or operational history. Leadership content remains omitted until approved names, roles and images are supplied.

### Global partnerships

Explain white-label scope, asset handoff, confidentiality requirements, feedback owner, review rounds, export QA, localization review and project versus retainer arrangements. Treat SLAs and revision terms as items agreed in the proposal until an operational policy is approved.

### Contact

Support proposal, discovery-call and partnership intents. The scheduler uses an approved real booking URL. Without one, offer **Request a Discovery Call** through the form and explain that a time will be arranged after review; do not present it as an instantly booked appointment.
